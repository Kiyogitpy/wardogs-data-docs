# Wardogs - Ranged Weapon Item Fields

> **Project:** [`Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs) - [Live site](https://kiyogitpy.github.io/wardogs-data-docs/)

The item-level settings that sit next to a weapon's stats asset: the `WDItemExtensionData_RangedWeapon` block of each weapon item and the crosshair override of the `WDItemExtensionData_Holdable` block. Nothing here was measured in the game. Labels: **Data** for what the export says, **Not traced** where no reader of the field was found.

A dash means the item does not set the field (an exported asset leaves out every property that equals the class default). Spread, recoil and sway values are on [Weapon Spread and Recoil Data](Wardogs_WeaponSpreadRecoilData_v1.md); attachment effects are on [Weapon Attributes](Wardogs_WeaponAttributes_v1.md). Regenerate the tables with `python -I _tools/weapon_ranged_fields.py --write`.

What each column holds (**Data**):

- **Rechamber after each shot** is `bRequiresRechamberAfterFire`. Seven items set it: the 9K333 Verba, AMR 50, M500, MK22, Mosin Nagant, RPG-7 and SV98. The set is the bolt-action rifles, the M500 and the single-shot launchers; no semi-auto or full-auto weapon sets it.
- **Rechamber delay** is `TimeAfterFireToRechamber` in seconds. It is set on eleven items, including weapons that do not require a rechamber (SVD, PKM, MP43, M14, TCX-SPR, Evo-3 Ultra, Judge), so the delay is not limited to bolt guns. **Not traced:** which code starts the rechamber animation after the delay. Round 92 searched the native functions that fetch this data block (the 24 callers of its class getter `0x6C8EAD0` and the 9 callers of the item-data getter `0x6A7E0E0`) for a read of `+0x290` and found none, so the delay is probably consumed in Blueprint. The flag `bRequiresRechamberAfterFire` (`+0x28D`) is read natively once, in an extension check (`0x6CA0A70`, vtable slot 191, test at `0x6CA0B7E`) that is not the rechamber timer.
- **Unchamber on reload** is `bShouldUnchamberDuringReload`; only the Judge and the MGL-40 set it.
- **Iron-sight camera offset** and **sight position offset** are vectors in the weapon's local axes, units not stated by the export (the values run from 0.03 to 10.5, so the two fields use different scales). They are per weapon and not per sight. **Native (round 92):** the first-person weapon animation instance copies them every update; no camera code reads them directly.

  - The iron-sight camera offset (data `+0x270`) is copied into the animation instance's `SightCameraOffset` (instance `+0x1228`) in the per-frame update `0x6A80880` (load `0x6A80BD8`, store `0x6A80BF0`) and when the held item changes (`0x6A80060`, store `0x6A801D8`). When the attached sight sets `bOverrideSightCameraOffset` (sight struct `+0x30`), the same update replaces it with `(0, v, 0)`, where `v` is the sight's per-weapon value (map at sight struct `+0x38`, keyed by the weapon's item tag) or its default `SightCameraOffsetOverride` (`+0x34`) (`unpcklpd` at `0x6A81531`, store at `0x6A8153C`). So the per-weapon vector is the offset only for sights that do not override it, and an overriding sight can only move the camera along the Y axis.
  - The sight position offset (data `+0x240`) is copied into the animation instance's `SightPositionOffset` (`+0xC20`) when a sight is attached, by `0x6A89880` (store `0x6A8991F`) and again on attachment replication by `0x6A89A90`. Its Y part is then replaced by a byte of the weapon extension (`+0x150`) when that byte is non-zero (`0x6A89951`). The first-person animation update `0x6A81D10` (vtable slot 121) then subtracts it, component by component, from a vector built from socket transforms, the sight socket among them (`0x6A82139`, `0x6A82142`, `0x6A8214B`) and stores the difference as `SightAttachmentOffset` (`+0x13B0`) and `RootToSightOffset` (`+0x13C8`). The grip position offset (data `+0x258`) goes to instance `+0xC68` in `0x6A88D20` (store `0x6A88FB9`).
  - **Still open:** what moves the camera or the weapon with `SightCameraOffset`, `SightAttachmentOffset` and `RootToSightOffset`. In the animation and weapon code (RVA `0x6000000` to `0x7900000`) the only functions that touch `+0x1228` are the writers `0x6A7E2E0`, `0x6A80060` and `0x6A80880`, and the only ones that touch `+0x13B0` and `+0x13C8` are `0x6A7E2E0` and `0x6A81D10`, all writers. The final use is therefore in the animation Blueprint, which is not in the corpus.
- **Fire blend targets** (`FireAnimBlendTargets`) is 5 on the AK74 and Galil and 10 on the AMR 50, Compound Bow, MK22, Mosin Nagant and SV98. **Buffered fire blend** (`BufferedFireAnimBlendTime`) is 0 on five items and 0.05 s on the Compound Bow. **Native (round 92):** both feed one animation message. After a shot, the extension's handler `0x6C9C000` (an interface method reached through vtable slot 201; its caller was not traced) picks a blend time and sends a `WDItemAnimationMessage` carrying that blend time and `FireAnimBlendTargets` as the message's blend-target byte (`0x6C9C2E1`; default 15 when none is set).

  - **Blend time.** `FireAnimBlendTimeCurve` (data `+0x148`) is evaluated at the owner's clock minus the last-shot stamp held in the weapon component (`+0xFE8`, written by the fire function `0x6B93610` at `0x6B94597`). Whether the stamp is already updated for the current shot when this handler runs was not traced, so the input may be the time since the previous shot or close to 0 (code `0x6C9C2B4` to `0x6C9C2D7`); a result above 0 is used. The blend option sent is the constant 3 (the engine's `Sinusoidal` blend option; `0x6C9C2F1`). `FireAnimBlendOption` (`+0x1D4`) has no reader among the native functions that fetch the data block, so the data value is not what is sent.
  - **Buffered fire.** `BufferedFireAnimBlendTime` is stored in the extension (`+0x3C4`) when a fire action is queued in the action buffer (event type 4 `BufferQueued`, handler `0x6CA1840`, load at `0x6CA197C`) and cleared when the queued action is cancelled (type 5). If that stored value is above 0 when the handler next runs, it replaces the curve value and is cleared (`0x6C9C29B` to `0x6C9C2AF`). A value of 0 therefore turns the buffered case off; only the Compound Bow sets a non-zero value.
  - **Targets.** The message listener (the Holdable extension, registered in `0x6C6FF40`, callback `0x6C6E3D0`) passes the byte to `0x6C6FB20`, which treats it as a bit mask. A set bit applies the message's blend time to one animation, a clear bit leaves that animation at its own default blend: bit 1 the first-person player animation, bit 2 the third-person player animation, bit 4 the first-person item animation, bit 8 the third-person item animation. So the value 5 (AK74, Galil) blends the first-person player and weapon animations only, and the value 10 (AMR 50, Compound Bow, MK22, Mosin Nagant, SV98) blends the third-person ones only. An item that sets no targets has 0, which blends nothing. The mapping of bits to animations is read from the order of the message fields and the calls in `0x6C6FB20`, not confirmed in play.
- **Trace radius** is `WeaponTraceRadius`, 3 on pistols and the Judge, 4 on the Deagle, 6 to 7 on rifles, 8 on the Verba. The M500 sets none.
- The two vehicle barrel test items are left out.

## Crosshair overrides on holdable items

Every holdable can replace the default crosshair. Twenty-four items do (**Data**): throwables use the `Throwable` crosshair type, the three hammers and the Halligan Bar use `BuildTool` with a buildable check radius of 275 cm (350 cm for the Large Hammer), and the repair tools and fuel canister use a plain cross with hair length 7.5, gap 5 and opacity 0.6. The remote detonator draws a dot only. No weapon in the table above overrides its crosshair; the weapons use the default crosshair, which the spread settings drive.

## Standalone weapon curves

Seven `CurveFloat` assets sit in the weapon plugins (**Data**). Five belong to bipods and give a camera location offset by field of view (the key runs from 40 to 120, apparently a field of view in degrees; the unit of the value is not stated). Two are ADS-in progress curves for the Galil and the FAL: the FAL one is a straight line, the Galil one stays at 0 until 29% of the ADS time and then rises linearly to 1. **Round 92:** the native bipod code copies the curve but no native function evaluates it. The bipod attachment struct holds it as `CameraLocationFOVOffsetCurve` (struct `+0x60`). It is copied into the bipod character modifier (`0x6E94FB0`, call `0x6E95164`), the bipod mover mode (`0x6DBC390`, call `0x6DBC52D`) and a third state object (`0x6DC47A0`). A scan of every native function that type-checks bipod data (the seven callers of `0x69A0F80`) and of the direct calls in the bipod modifier and mover-mode vtable slots found no curve evaluation. The camera-lock modifiers (slot 92, `0x6E96D90`, calling `0x6E979B0`; curve read at `0x6E97D65`) do evaluate curves, but from a normalised progress value, so they are probably lock-easing curves and not `CameraLocationFOVOffsetCurve`; the curve's source in that function was not traced. None of those seven functions is a camera modifier. Which code reads the curve is **Not traced** (probably the animation or control-rig Blueprint).

## Magazine price points by ammunition type

Each magazine's vendor row holds seven variations (30 of 33 magazines), one per ammunition type: the shown "standard" price, then FMJ, hollow point and armor piercing, each with a tracer twin. Only the first has `bShowInVendor` set, so the vendor list shows the standard price; the rest are the prices for loading that ammunition type (**Data**). **Native (round 92):** each variation is a complete purchasable entry: the purchase request names a variation tag, the vendor finds that row in the item's variation list (`0x6BCDB20`) and charges that row's own `Price` (`+0xE0`) times a factor held by the requesting object (`+0x39C`, load at `0x762E3B8` in the purchase handler `0x762E0A0`). The same row's `MagazinePackingData` custom data (struct `+0x178`) is expanded when the item is granted (`0x6BD6DB0`, `0x739FE50`, helper `0x6BED650`) into the magazine plus its ammunition: each pattern entry's quantity, capped at the pattern quantity and at the magazine's capacity. So a variation price is the price of buying that magazine already loaded with that ammunition type, not a surcharge applied on top of the standard price. Whether the vendor UI shows the non-standard rows is **not traced** (they have `bShowInVendor` false). Three magazines have fewer: one lacks the tracer variants and two list only the tracer types. Every variation unlocks at level 1.

## Transfer time on the 9K333 Verba

The 9K333 Verba ([`ID_Launcher_04`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Weapons/Launchers/Launcher_04/Content/Data/ID_Launcher_04.json)) is the only item with a `WDInventoryFragment_TransferTime` fragment: `TransferTime` = **3.5 s**. The inventory transfer rule has a default transfer time of 0.5 s ([Game Mechanics](Wardogs_GameMechanics_v2.md)). **Native (round 92):** the fragment replaces the rule default. The transfer rule's native body (`UWDCustomInventoryOperationRule_Transfer`, vtable slot 86, `0x6C4F5C0`) starts from the rule's `DefaultTransferTime` (`+0x28`, load at `0x6C4F9E7`). If the moved item's data has a `WDInventoryFragment_TransferTime` (fetched by `0x6C4F1C0`), its `TransferTime` (`+0x38`, `0x6C4FA1D`) replaces that value. When the operation has a second item (flag at operation `+0x32`) and that item has a `WDInventoryFragment_TransferTime`, the larger of its `TransferTime` and the value already held (the first item's fragment time, or the rule default if the first item has none) is used (`maxss` at `0x6C4FA63`). If the second item has no fragment, the value is unchanged. The result is passed on as the operation's duration. The Verba therefore takes 3.5 s where other items take the rule default.
