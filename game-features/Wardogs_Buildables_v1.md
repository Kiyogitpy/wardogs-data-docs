## 1. How a buildable is put together

A player builds with a **hammer** (Small, Medium or Large). The hammer's table says which buildables it can place. Placing makes a **schematic** (a see-through ghost). Building then moves it through stages until it is finished. Hammers need **Build Supplies** to work (**Data**: the item descriptions in section 7).

| Piece | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `WDBuildableDataAsset` | The main definition: tag, supply cost, health, placement rules, damage row, sounds, wheel slot | one `DA_Buildable_*` per structure or part |
| `WDActorBuildSpawner` | A small sub-object inside each definition. It names the actor class that is created (`B_Buildable_C` for most structures) | 32 of them, one per definition |
| `BuildingStagesDefinition` | The build stages: a progress threshold, the meshes shown and the weak spots (critical-hit spots) per stage | one `*_Stages` per definition |
| Radial wheel slot | The icon and title in the build wheel | `DA_RadialWheelSlotType_*` |
| Damage row | What each damage type does to the structure | `DT_GlobalDamage_BuildableData` |

**Data.** The special spawner classes are: `B_Buildable_BarbedWire_C`, `B_Buildable_CamoNetTent_C`, `B_Buildable_Radio_C`, `B_Buildable_BremmerWall_C`, `B_Buildable_HBlock_QuadWall_C`, `B_BuildableRefuelStation_C`, `B_BuildableRepairStation_C`, `B_Buildable_HotzoneMagnet_C`, `B_Buildable_CrowsNestActorReplacing_C` and `B_Buildable_Door_ActorReplacing_C`. The Forward Operating Base picks a class by faction (section 8).

**Data.** The feature root [`Buildables`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Items/Buildables/Content/Buildables.json) adds a pawn component (`WDPawnBuildableManager`) to the player character and a vehicle component (`WDVehicleBuildableComponent`) to stationary vehicles. It also registers the vendor table, the radial wheel registry and the build tool HUD widget (`W_BuildTool`).

## 2. Inventory

One row per definition (32 rows). Parts of a bigger structure (floor, roof, gate halves) are separate definitions and cannot be picked from the wheel alone.

- **Supply cost** is the `Cost` field (**Data**). The game code has three cost getters on the asset: `GetPlacementCost`, `GetTotalBuildCost` and `GetTotalSupplyCost` (**Native**, reflection thunks at RVA `0x8593E80`, `0x8593FB0`, `0x85940E0`). The first two read a float scale from the player's object. How `Cost` becomes Build Supplies spent, and whether it is taken at placement or while building, is **not traced**.
- **Name** is `DisplayName`, or the row in `DT_EntityData_UI_Buildables` when that is empty. `(blank in export)` means the text key exists but its source string is empty in the export (WoodBase, Shelter and the Recon Tower parts). The UI name table gives the same name to HBlock, TallHBlock and HBlockQuadWall ("HBlock"), while the asset names are Hesco Block (Small), Hesco Block (Large) and Hesco Wall.
- **Repair multiplier** is `RepairSpeedMultiplier`. `-` means the field is not set. Meaning of the number beyond "bigger repairs faster" is **not traced**.
- **Hammers**: S, M, L = Small, Medium, Large (**Data**, the three `DT_Buildables_*Hammer` tables). Parts are placed together with their parent.

## 3. Build stages

Stages are progress thresholds from 0 to 1. A stage becomes visible when the build progress reaches its `RequiredBuildingProgress`. The export carries **no build time per stage**; build speed comes from the hammer ("Fast", "Moderate", "Slow" in the item text) and the construct action [`DA_Action_Buildable_Construct`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Items/Buildables/Content/Data/Actions/DA_Action_Buildable_Construct.json). The per-hit build amount and the swing animation times of each hammer are in the table below (**Data**, [round 17 evidence](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/docs/evidence/round17-blueprint-defaults.md)). Supplies spent per stage are not in the stage files.

**Hammer swing and build amount** (`buildable_tables hammer_speed`). Build amount per hit is `WDItemExtensionData_BuildTool.BuildAmount` on the hammer item; the critical-spot amount is `BuildAmountWhenTargetIsHit` (**Data**). The swing times are the first-person use montage: the swing loops between the `LoopStart` and `LoopEnd` sections while the button is held and the `Hit` notify marks the blow (**Data (Blueprint CDO)**).

**Derived**, if the loop section is the swing period and one `BuildAmount` is added per hit: Large 1.5 per 0.88 s (about 1.7 per second), Medium 1.0 per 0.57 s (about 1.76 per second), Small 0.5 per 0.57 s (about 0.88 per second). That puts Large and Medium about equal, although the shop text calls the Large hammer fast and the Medium hammer moderate. So either the swing period is not the loop length, or the scale of `BuildAmount` against the 0 to 1 stage progress is set elsewhere (the buildable's own build total is native and not decoded). The native defaults page lists `BuildAmount` 3.0 and `BuildAmountPerSecond` 3.0 for the separate extension class `UWDItemExtensionData_BuildBuildable`; whether the hammer path uses it is not traced. The large hammer also carries `DismantleRatePerSecond` 250, medium 200, small 150 (equal to the per-hit values).

- Most structures use 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1. The Door uses 0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.8, 1 and the WoodBase uses 0, 0.2, 0.45, 0.7, 1.
- Parts (floors, roofs, gate halves, door halves) have one stage at 1, so they appear complete.
- The last stage has no weak spots (critical-hit spots) in every multi-stage definition. Earlier stages carry them, so a half-built structure has marked weak spots (what the weak spots do is **not traced** on this page).
- `bOverridesFinalStage` is set on 6 definitions: Indirect Fire Shelter, Door, Bunker, Recon Tower, Gate and Hesco Wall (**Data**). What it changes is **not traced**. The Door and Recon Tower use `...ActorReplacing` spawner classes.
- The Forward Operating Base has no stage file; it is `bInstantFinish` (**Data**).

## 4. Health and damage rules

`MaxHealth` is set per definition. The `DamageEntityData` tag points at one row of [`DT_GlobalDamage_BuildableData`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Items/Buildables/Content/Data/DT_GlobalDamage_BuildableData.json). There are 20 rows. Every definition points at one, and every row is used.

How to read a percentage (**Native**, [damage pipeline section 6](../pipelines/Wardogs_DamagePipeline_v2.md)): the operation is `Percentage_Ongoing`, so each matching entry changes the running damage `C` by `Amount * 0.01 * C`. `-80%` means 20% of the damage is left. `+1400%` means 15 times the damage. Which entries are selected, and in what order, is in the same pipeline page (rule tables, entry selection and ordering). This confirms the sign reading that the Items page marked as inferred.

The table shows the entries that match one source tag alone (no instigator, target or hit-location requirement). Other entries exist (armor-piercing and hollow-point bullets, many rocket and cannon calibers, throwables) and are in the [Items page section 6.3](Wardogs_GameFeatures_Items_v2.md) and the data file. `-` means that source has no single-tag entry in that row.

## 5. Placement rules

All of this is **Data**. Global limits live in [`DA_BuildablePlacementData`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Items/Buildables/Content/Data/Buildables/DA_BuildablePlacementData.json):

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Max distance to the placement point (`MaxPlaceTraceDistance`) | 600 |
| Max support height (`SupportHeightLimit`) | 1,500 |
| Valid / invalid ghost colour | white / red, both at alpha 64 (`40FFFFFF`, `40FF0000`) |
| Actors ignored when a foundation is blocked | `LandscapeStreamingProxy`, `PCGPartitionActor` |
| Actors ignored when a snapped placement is blocked | `LandscapeStreamingProxy`, `WDDroppedItem` |

Per-structure rules are in the table that follows. Plain meaning:

- **Placement conditions** are checked before a ghost turns valid. `SafeZonePlacementCondition` and `WithinLadderBoundsPlacementCondition` are Blueprint conditions (names only; what they test is **not traced**). `WDWithinFobBoundsPlacementCondition` means the spot must be inside a friendly FOB radius. `WDNotInsideEnemyFobBoundsPlacementCondition` forbids it inside an enemy FOB radius. `WDLimitedByFOBCondition` enforces the per-FOB limit. `WDFobPlacementCondition` is the FOB's own rule. `WDPlacementZoneCondition` and `WDWithinControlZonePlacementCondition` limit the Drill Rig to zones.
- **Small structures** (Barbed Wire, Recon Tent, Sandbag Wall, Hedgehog) do not need a FOB. Every other structure except the FOB itself must be inside a FOB radius (**Derived**: from which conditions each definition lists).
- **Max stack** is the `MaxStack` field. Its meaning (a placement count or a snap-stack height) is **not traced**; `0` is probably "no stack allowed" but this is unconfirmed.
- **FOB limit** is the per-FOB count from [`DA_FOB_Ruleset`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Items/Buildables/Content/Data/Buildables/ForwardOperatingBase/DA_FOB_Ruleset.json): Refuel Station 1, Repair Station 1, Builder's Radio 4.
- `PlacementFlags` is 9 for almost every structure and 1 for the FOB. The bit meaning is **not traced**.
- Terrain: `MaximumValidSurfaceAngle` is 10 on the Gate parts; `MaxDepthUnderTerrain` lets a structure sink 30 to 200 into the ground; `bRequiresAllCornersOnSurface` is false and `bRotateWithSurface` is true on the small walls.

## 8. The Forward Operating Base

The FOB is the anchor for most other structures. Facts (**Data**):

- Item description: "Needed to Build Larger Structures. Can hold Items & Resources for Players to Use. Provides a Radius in Which Players Can Build."
- Vendor price 7,500 for one FOB item. Buildable definition: 15,000 health, `Cost` 30, `PlacementCost` 0, repair multiplier 2, instant finish.
- One class per faction: Alpha uses `B_Buildable_ForwardOperatingBase_Lonestar_C`, Bravo `..._Valkyra_C`, Charlie and the default `..._Manticore_C`. Each has its own placement class.
- Limits inside one FOB: 1 Refuel Station, 1 Repair Station, 4 Builder's Radios.
- Two interactions ([`DA_FOB_Interactions`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/data/Wardogs/Plugins/GameFeatures/Items/Buildables/Content/Data/Buildables/ForwardOperatingBase/DA_FOB_Interactions.json)): Access (tap on keyboard, hold on gamepad, several users at once) and Hacking (hold 30 seconds, one user, success and failure sent to the server).
- Sounds exist for deposits of Ammo, Build, Fuel and Mechanical supplies, pallets and supply crates, and for hack start, stop, success and fail (laptop and siren variants).
- Cash for hacking and closing a FOB door is in the [Rewards page](Wardogs_GameFeatures_Rewards_v2.md).

A spawn point is **not** found in this plugin. If the FOB acts as a spawn location, that comes from another feature and is **not traced**.

## 9. Cash and XP

Placing, building and destroying are paid through the reward system. The flat cash tables are `DT_Reward_Cash_BuildableBuilt`, `DT_Reward_Cash_BuildablePlaced`, `DT_Reward_Cash_BuildablePlacedInFOB` and, for destroying an enemy structure, the buildable rows of `DT_Reward_Cash_DeployableDestroyed`. All values are **Data**.

- **Built** is a flat 1 cash (a placeholder-sized credit), so the real payout is **Placed**.
- **Placed** pays the owner (owner modifier 1) and **Placed in FOB** pays 0 to the owner (owner modifier 0) with the same base. How the two tables are chosen between is **not traced**.
- A friendly modifier of -0.75 applies to placed and destroyed rows.
- Only 10 structures have a Placed value. The Sandbag Wall, Door, Recon Tower, Recon Tent and Drill Rig have a Destroyed value only. WoodBase and Shelter have no cash row in these tables.
