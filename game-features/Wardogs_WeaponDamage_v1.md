This page gives the damage one bullet or pellet does per hit, for every infantry weapon in the export that fires bullets or pellets. It covers FMJ, tracer, hollow point (HP) and armor-piercing (AP) ammo, and the rankings that answer "which weapon does the most damage with a given ammo". Sections 1 to 4 are point blank direct hits, unarmored or against the armor class named in each table. Section 5 adds damage over distance, section 6 applies armor at every hit location, and section 7 gives the ricochet and penetration constants and what the export cannot tell about walls. The short answer: AP does less than FMJ against unarmored targets and more against heavy armor; see the rankings.

## 2. How AP, HP and FMJ differ

The three ammo types are the same bullet with a different modifier. All numbers below are from the table in section 3 and the rule notes in [`round24-weapon-damage-engine.md`](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/docs/evidence/round24-weapon-damage-engine.md).

**Against no armor, AP is worse than FMJ.** Every weapon with AP ammo deals 0.8 times the FMJ damage to an unarmored torso. The M4 does 21.8 with AP against 27.3 with FMJ. AP removes part of the armor reduction, and it lowers damage when there is no armor to remove.

**Against armor, AP is better than FMJ, but only from a certain class.** The armor class scales the damage down by less for AP than for FMJ, so AP catches up and passes FMJ:

- Rifle and heavier calibers (556 mm, 545 mm, 762 mm, 762x54 mm, 308 Win) overtake FMJ at Medium. The M4 does 16.6 with AP and 16.4 with FMJ at Medium. The FAL does 21.0 against 19.7. At SuperHeavy the gap is larger: the M4 does 14.0 against 10.9, and the FAL 18.4 against 13.1.
- Pistol calibers (9 mm, 45 ACP, 45 Colt) do not overtake at Medium. The Glock 17 does 14.6 with AP and 15.2 with FMJ at Medium. They overtake at Heavy: the Glock 17 does 13.2 against 12.7, and the Judge 27.0 against 26.0.
- The Deagle (50 AE) is a pistol caliber that overtakes FMJ at Medium: 46.6 against 43.7.

**Hollow point is the reverse.** HP does 2.0 times the FMJ damage to an unarmored torso (the M4 does 54.5 against 27.3). It falls to 0.36 times FMJ at Medium and 0.13 times at SuperHeavy. Two things cause the collapse: the HP rule entries that cut damage by 70, 55, 40 and 40 percent against the armor classes, and an armor scalar of 1.5 that makes armor count 1.5 times. The M4 does 5.9 with HP at Medium and 1.5 at SuperHeavy. On the head, HP does 131.6 with no helmet and 5.9 with a Heavy helmet.

**The .50 cal SR_04 is the exception for AP.** Its arms class gives the AP armor scalar a value of 1.0, the same as FMJ, so AP never beats FMJ on this weapon. It does 166.3 with AP against 207.9 with FMJ with no armor, and 58.2 against 72.8 at SuperHeavy. The ratio is 0.8 at every armor class. It still ranks first for AP because its base damage is 90 per round, the highest of any weapon with AP.

## 5. Damage over distance

Damage at distance d is the point blank damage times a distance curve times a speed scale: `damage(d) = point-blank damage x curve(d) x speed_scale(d)`. The point blank damage already holds the ammo and armor rules, so the multiplier depends on the weapon and ammo only, not on armor or location. The multiplier is the curve, where the weapon applies one, times the speed scale. The model is stored at 14 distances from 0 to 1500 m; this section shows 100, 400, 800 and 1200 m. Distances are measured from the muzzle along the line of sight. Gravity, wind and spread are left out (see the evidence doc for the sensitivity checks).

The speed scale is `clamp(speed / reference, 0, 1)`. The reference is the weapon's `TypicalSpeed` where the weapon overrides it, and otherwise the projectile asset's `TypicalSpeed`. So the scale applies to every weapon, not only the ones that override the speed. The muzzle speed is above the reference for every row, so the scale is 1 at the muzzle. The first point where it falls below 1 ranges from 35 m (M1911) to 2016 m (AMR 50), and the heavy rounds (SV98, MK22, SVD, Mosin Nagant, PKM) keep a scale of 1 until about 900 to 1150 m. The speed comes from a drag-only flight integrated in 1 ms steps. Two choices move it slightly. Solving for an elevated shot that lands at the muzzle height changes the scale by under 1 percent at 200 and 400 m for rifles; the largest change found is 22 percent, for the GGX 17 at 1200 m (0.18 on the line of sight, 0.22 elevated). The game steps the flight by `floor(1000 / Rate)` ms, and the Rate is not read; 16 ms steps change the scale by at most 2.2 percent (M500 buckshot at 800 m). Tracer, HP and AP rows use the base projectile asset, because the modifier values the game applies to those rounds are not in the export.

## 6. Armor on every hit location

Sections 1 to 4 evaluate armor at Torso.Upper (vests) and Head (helmets). The armor rule keys on the hit location. An armor item applies only where its `CoveredHitLocations` lists that location, and any other armor is removed from the calculation. For FMJ the damage factor is 1 minus R/100, where R is the item's reduction. The M4 shows the effect: a Torso.Middle hit into a Heavy vest takes 13.6, not the 27.2 unarmored, and a Neck hit into a Heavy helmet takes 24.7, not 49.4.

## 7. Ricochet and penetration

Sections 1 to 6 treat a bullet as stopping at its first hit. The export holds the constants for a bullet that bounces off or passes through a surface. It does not hold the physical material of any world wall or prop, so this section describes each ammo type against each surface type and cannot give a result for a specific wall.

**Determined in the export or traced in code:**

- The constants of 63 surface types: exit-speed modifier, ricochet probability and restitution range (data).
- The ammo modifiers: penetration effectiveness, exit speed, effective range and tracer chance (data).
- The pass condition: a surface is passed when the bullet's capacity is greater than the thickness divided by the cosine of the impact angle, and the thickness is at least 0.01 (traced).
- The speed lost in a pass, and the damage factor at the next impact (traced).
- Ricochet is tried before penetration, so a hit that ricochets is not also penetrated (**New, unverified**).
- A bullet with penetration effectiveness of 2 or more cannot ricochet. Between 2 and 1 the ricochet term ramps up, and it is 1 at 1 or below (**New, unverified**).

**Not determined:**

- The physical material, density and thickness of any world wall or prop. The export has 156 data files with a `PhysicalMaterialOverride`; 9,076 entries are `null` (the mesh default, which is not exported) and only 125 name a material.
- The unit of thickness, so every load in this section is dimensionless.
- The exact ricochet reflection, the deflection and the ratio gate. They are decoded from unreviewed pseudocode (**New, unverified**).
- The hit limit. The history bitmap is 32 bits wide, so hits 2 to 33 can be marked; what happens past hit 33 is not determined.
- The exit deflection after a pass and its effect on a target behind a wall.
- Pellet spread behind a pass. The shotgun rows use the per-pellet formulas only.
- Any armor interaction after a pass. None was found.

### Speed after a pass

The formulas below are as the evidence doc states them, with the flags it gives. The capacity, pass and speed formulas are traced in native code. The ricochet decision that comes before a pass is **New and unverified**. The exit deflection distribution is not re-derived.

- Capacity: `v x 0.01 x m x c x E / (rho x 0.099999994 x d^2)`, where v is the impact speed, m the projectile mass, d its diameter, c the cosine of the impact angle, E the penetration effectiveness of the ammo and rho the larger density of the two materials.
- Pass: `capacity > T / c` and `T >= 0.01`. T is the thickness along the ray, in an engine unit that is not established.
- Speed after a pass: `v x (1 - T / capacity) x clamp(1 - 0.5 (p + s), 0, 1)`, followed by a random exit deflection. p is the ammo's exit-speed multiplier and s the surface's exit-speed modifier.
- Damage at the next impact: `clamp(v_after / reference, 0, 1)`. The current impact keeps its pre-exit speed, so the damage after a pass is scaled at the next hit, not this one.

The load `f = T / capacity` is the speed loss. The JSON writes the capacity coefficient as `K = v x 0.01 x m x E / (0.099999994 x d^2)`, so the capacity is `K c / rho`.

## Limitations

- **Hostile alignment, target outside any vehicle, no instigator tags.** Entries that need an instigator tag are counted as unknown and not applied.
- **Distance, armor and penetration values are a model.** Gravity is left out, the shot runs along the line of sight, and there is no wind, spread, pellet separation or target height. Sections 5 to 7 were derived from the data and the documented rules; no live game observation was made. The Judge and the shotguns follow the data as stated in section 5.
- **Vendor availability is not modeled.** The ammo a weapon can load is taken from the ammo items whose caliber the weapon supports. Whether the vendor shows an item (`bShowInVendor`) is not used. Some variants are hidden from the vendor, and what that means in play is not determined. See the [ammo availability evidence](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/docs/evidence/round24-ammo-availability.md).
- **Rate of fire** is shown only where the weapon's stats asset sets `RoundsPerMinute`. The LMG (PKM) and the Glock 17 do not set it, so no value is given for them. The class default is not applied.
- **Excluded weapons.** Launchers (RPG-7, MAAWS, MGL-40, 9K333 Verba, the underbarrel grenade launcher and the AT4 asset) fire rockets or grenades, not bullets. The combat bow fires arrows. Fists, the build tools and the hammers are not weapons with a caliber. The artillery and tank barrel tests, the mounted M2 Browning and the vehicle weapons are excluded as well. See the [weapon damage evidence](https://github.com/Kiyogitpy/wardogs-data-docs/blob/main/docs/evidence/round24-weapon-damage-engine.md).
- **Items that are not determined.** These are listed in the evidence docs and are not settled by the game files:
  - What the Ghillie suit does. Its armor items carry no stages and no covered locations, so the tables treat it as no armor. The damage tables do hold Ghillie rows (Armor entry 7 at -10000 percent, Helmet entry 6 at -1000 percent), and the suit's `AppliedEffects` are not in the export, so its real effect is not determined.
  - The `ArmourPiercing` item tag (the .338 Norma AP rounds) is not in the ammo-modifier map, so those rounds behave as FMJ. No weapon in the export chambers .338 Norma, so no row shows this.
  - The `545mm.Tracer` item has the caliber tag but no `Item.Ammo` context. Whether that stops it from loading is not determined. The table includes it.
  - Which vehicle weapons load 7.62x51 mm, .338 Norma, 20x102 mm, 30 mm, 35x228 mm and the tank calibers is not traced.
  - Whether a magazine or chamber loads an item with no `Item.Ammo` context, and what loot or world spawns offer ammo, is not traced.
  - The density, thickness and surface type of world walls and props. The export has no physical-material assignment for any world mesh, so no per-wall or per-prop figure exists (section 7).
  - The unit of wall thickness, so the ricochet and penetration loads are dimensionless.
  - The ricochet reflection, the deflection and the ratio gate. They are decoded from unreviewed pseudocode and marked New in section 7.
  - The hit limit for a projectile past hit 33, and the exit deflection after a pass.
  - The game's step length for flight. The model steps 1 ms; the game steps by `floor(1000 / Rate)` ms, and the Rate is not read. If the Rate is the config's `SimulationFrameRate` of 30 (33 ms steps), a plain Euler step would move the distance where the speed scale begins to bite by up to about 10 percent (M500 buckshot from 92 m to 83 m; 2.5 percent at 16 ms steps). That scheme is an exploratory check, not the game's, so every cell with a speed scale below 1 carries this uncertainty.
  - Native speed modifiers on modifier ammo. Tracer, HP and AP rows use the base projectile asset.
  - The M500 and MP43 curve enable flag. The stored shotgun curve is not applied in the model, and whether the game applies it is not determined.
  - Explosive and radial hits ignore location coverage: their armor hook accepts every equipped piece (round 8). The location tables cover direct bullet hits only.
  - Pilot and Tank helmets: the armor rule reaches them through the helmet parent tag, which is inferred and not checked in a live hit. Whether two vests or two helmets can be worn together is assumed not, not read.
- **Odd numbers in the data.** The Scout Rifle TD (WEPN_035) fires 556 mm but does 73.9 per torso hit with FMJ, against 27.3 for the M4. The gap comes from its sniper weapon factor (x2.64) in the damage rules. The values are what the rules produce, not a tested result.
